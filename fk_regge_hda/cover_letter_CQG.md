Please consider the manuscript **“Fourth-Order Restoration of the Canonical Gauge Sector in Refined Four-Dimensional Regge Calculus”** for publication as a Research Paper in *Classical and Quantum Gravity*.

Generic finite Regge discretizations break the vertex-displacement symmetry of continuum gravity, replacing exact constraints by pseudo-constraints. Earlier work established the relevant Bianchi identities, approximate conservation relations at small deficit angle, and quadratic lifting of would-be gauge Hessian modes. The present manuscript asks a narrower canonical question: how does this known small-curvature mechanism propagate through physical/gauge reduction and the old/new tent-move Legendre map under shape-regular four-dimensional refinement?

For a fully stationary ordinary length-Regge Freudenthal–Kuhn two-step family containing 48 four-simplices, we find a sharp sectoral hierarchy. The four middle-slice gauge singular values scale as O(h^4) while the twelve-dimensional physical gap remains O(1). More importantly, the directly evaluated old/new mixed Lagrangian Hessian satisfies K_gg=O(h^4) in the geometric deformation sector, while gauge-to-physical mixing remains K_gp=O(h^2). The stationary response is bounded on boundary deformation directions but behaves as approximately h^-2 for unrestricted physical boundary forcing. Thus the result is not a claim that the entire discrete canonical map becomes fourth-order gauge invariant.

The analytic argument traces the fourth-order scale to the contracted Regge Bianchi identity and a Schur-corrected obstruction,

G_4^eff = P_L (H_4 - H_2 H_0^+ H_2) P_R,

under explicit shape-regularity, stationarity, physical-gap, and fourth-order transversality hypotheses. Independently, neighboring FK normal-deformation commutators recover the expected inverse-metric structure function, and a fixed-volume weak assembly preserves the fourth-order deformation-sector rate.

We have deliberately bounded the claim. The theorem concerns near-flat, on-shell Euclidean **length Regge calculus** and the gauge/deformation block. It does not assert exact finite-spacing diffeomorphism symmetry, an unrestricted off-shell Dirac algebra, or a corresponding theorem for area Regge calculus. Lorentzian deformation kinematics is checked separately; a fully stationary Lorentzian canonical extension is left for future work.

We believe the work is particularly appropriate for *Classical and Quantum Gravity* because it directly connects Regge calculus, canonical gravity, discrete diffeomorphism symmetry, hypersurface-deformation structure, and refinement—topics with a substantial history in the journal.
